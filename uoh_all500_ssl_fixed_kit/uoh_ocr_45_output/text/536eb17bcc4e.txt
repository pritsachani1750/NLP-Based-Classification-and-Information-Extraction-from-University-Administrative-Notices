I. The meeting commenced with the Chairman extending a warm welcome to
the members attending the special meeting convened at short notice in the
context of recent significant developments like the Government of
Telangana deciding to auction 400 acres of land resumed from IMG
Academies Bharata, protests that erupted following Government’s
announcement, and TGIIC’s inconsistent clarifications to the media and
public on the issue.

The Vice-Chancellor briefed the Council on the important events concerning
the land issue:

1. The Vice-Chancellor took the Council through the chronology of events,
highlighting the key points culminating with the recent protests by
various stakeholders. He gave a quick brief on the allocation of 2300
acres of land free of cost in the Kancha Gachibowli village by the State
Government to establish the University way back in 1975
communicated through a letter from the Dy. Secretary Department of
Education. This allotment included 200 acres earmarked for the
Department of Science and Technology, Government of India.
Subsequently, the Government of Andhra Pradesh handed over Ac.
2324.05 Gts. of land to establish the University of Hyderabad in 1975,
but no alienation was done formally conferring the title to it in the last
50 years despite the best efforts of the University administration. He
further briefed on the significant developments in land matters to date.

2. At the request of the State Government, the Executive Council inter alia
decided to alienate 400 acres of land in Sy. No.25 in 2003 to the
Government of Andhra Pradesh/Sports Authority of Andhra Pradesh
(SAAP) to establish a Sports Academy. The land, originally allocated to
IMG Bharata, was transferred from the University of Hyderabad to the
State Government under a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU),
which, among other things, provided for the exchange of land. As per
records available, vide the District Collector’s proceedings dt.
31/01/2004, permission was accorded to resume the land to an extent
of Ac. 534.28 Gts. from University of Hyderabad in survey no. 25
situated in the Kancha Gachibowli Village, Serilingampally mandal and
handover advance possession of lands to an extent of Ac. 400-00 Gts.
to SAAP immediately for establishing International Sports Academy. The
Balance land Ac. 134.28 Gts. in Sy. No. 25 Kancha Gachibowli Village
was reserved in the Custody of Mandal Revenue Officer, Serilingampally
for A.P. NGOs Employees housing society. In lieu of that Ac. 397.16 Gts.
in survey no. 36 and 37 of Gopanpally Village was allocated to University
of Hyderabad immediately as compensatory measure.

3. Thus, the State Government handed over AC. 397.16 Gts of land to the
University towards partial fulfillment of terms and conditions set forth
in MoU. Although these 400 acres of land were handed over to the IMG
Academies Bharata Pvt. Ltd in 2004 by the State Government for the
construction of the Sports Academy, subsequently it was resumed by

Education and the Chief Minister of Government of Telangana from time

e The Counsel provided a comprehensive overview of the case, starting

rulings of the High Court and Supreme Court in the cases involving the

The Legal Counsel clarified that following the orders of the Hon’ble High
Court of Andhra Pradesh in WP No.24781 of 2006, and the orders of the
Supreme Court in SLP No. 9265 of 2024, the ownership of the 400 Acres of
land allotted to the university vests with the State Government, preventing
the university from obstructing the State Government authorities from
carrying out activities on this land. He also referred to arguments made by
the University and outcomes in WP No.816 of 2021 in this regard. However,
the Counsel noted that there is no physical demarcation of the 400 acres
on the ground. Given the practical considerations including built
infrastructure, environmental concerns, especially the water bodies and
rock formations on the said piece of land, the University may take up with
the State authorities to demarcate the land giving due regard to the
practical considerations, ecological balance and environmental concerns.

At this juncture, the Registrar intervened to clarify that contrary to the
TGIIC’s claims in its press release, the University has not agreed to any
demarcation of the land yet. University officials had accompanied State
Government and TGIIC representatives on a site visit to better understand
the practical considerations, topography and environmental concerns
involved.

He added that the site visit lasted only a few hours and the university has
not signed any document confirming the demarcation. The Registrar further
emphasized that the University has repeatedly informed the State
Government that, under the University of Hyderabad Act and Statutes, only
a duly constituted Executive Council has the authority to make decisions
regarding land matters. Therefore, the boundaries of University land cannot
be determined in such a casual manner, especially since no entity other
than the Executive Council has the legal competence to do so.

Continuing from where he stopped, the Standing Counsel further spelt out
the pros and cons of the different legal options available to the University
in the background of the Supreme Court’s decision to help the Council
make the best possible decision, considering the legal implications, risks,
and benefits of each option in the interests of the University.

IV. The Council also took note of the University’s extensive efforts to protect
its land, including legal recourses taken in the Hon’ble High Court and the
Supreme Court, whenever deemed necessary or when no other option
remained. The Council further acknowledged that the University had
sought assistance from the Ministry of Education and that the Hon’ble
Minister of Education, Shri Dharmendra Pradhan ji had written to the
Hon’ble Chief Minister of Telangana in 2022, requesting his intervention in
protecting the land allotted to the University. The Council expressed deep
gratitude to the Hon’ble Minister for his initiative and resolved to appeal to

The Council further noted that the University made forceful arguments in
several court cases, despite the challenge of formal documentation
establishing its right over the land allotted still remaining pending—the core
issue that significantly contributed to the setbacks in these cases. In this
context, the Counsel noted that while dismissing the Writ Petition No. 3502

The Council advised the administration to draw the attention of the State
Government to the above observations made by the Hon’ble High Court and
request the Government to adhere to these observations in both letter and
spirit and expeditiously conclude the Alienation Proposal submitted by the
Collector, RR District, in 2012.

V. Thereafter the Council deliberated extensively on land matters, specifically
focusing on the following points:

1. Legal Ownership of the 400 Acres of Land resumed by the State
Government from IMG Academies Bharata:

The Council acknowledged that, as a result of the protracted legal
proceedings culminating with the judgments of the Hon’ble High Court
and Supreme Court, the legal ownership of the 400 acres of land in
Sy.No.25 vests with the State Government. The land was originally
handed over to the State Government under the MoU, as per the
decision of the 126t Executive Council meeting held on 24/12/2003
which inter alia provided for land exchange. The Executive Council of
the University inter alia agreed to alienate the said extent of land,
subject to the Government agreeing to allot an equal extent of land to
the University as outlined in the MOU and considering favourably the
request regarding the preservation of biodiversity and natural habitat,
stated under V.4 below.”

The Council inquired from the Legal Counsel about the legal position
and the possibility of filing a writ petition. After considering the pros
and cons of various options, the Council resolved that, at this juncture,
the University should work closely with the Government to achieve the
objective of having the land alienated in favour of the University of
Hyderabad. The process of alienation of land in favour of the university
was initiated in 2012. Consequently, following the CCLA’s letter (Lr. No.

2. Engagement with University Alumni in Key Positions in Government:
The Council noted with pride that two distinguished alumni of the

resolving this long-standing issue of alienation of University land,
particularly during the Golden Jubilee year of the University, which has
marked several significant achievements in a short span of 50 years.

The University has consistently ranked among the top 20 universities
out of nearly 1,200 universities in the country. It has also earned the
prestigious status of Institution of Eminence (IoE), a recognition
awarded to only two other public universities: Delhi University and
BHU. With numerous other accolades, such as the first President’s
Award for the best University of India, the University has not only
fulfilled the very purpose for which it was established in Telangana
through an amendment to the Constitution of India (Article 371-E), as
part of the six-point formula but has also continuously brought pride
and glory to the State of Telangana. Not owning the land on which it has
been functioning is not tenable now for an Institute of Eminence.
Therefore, University EC strongly appealed to the good conscience of
Telangana Government to bestow the honor to UoH by giving the
ownership of the land on which it has been functioning gloriously for
the last 50 years.

emotional connection with the iconic Mushroom Rock and suggested
that, while being mindful of practical difficulties, the University may
work with State authorities to explore the possibility of retaining this

4. Protection of Biodiversity and Natural Habitat: The Executive Council

for the creation of infrastructure for IT and other industries. The Council
resolved to urge the State Government to take any action on this land

State Government may kindly keep these factors in view. It is pertinent

5. The Council expressed concern over the increasing attempts by private
parties, agencies, real estate companies, and other entities to encroach
upon University’s land. While these parties may not always use
fraudulent documents outright, there is a significant risk that they
could misuse legitimate University documents. This could include
taking valid documents out of context, omitting key information, or
selectively presenting them to create a misleading narrative before the
Court or other authorities to further their ulterior motives. The Council
discussed the potential for both fraudulent use and the improper
exploitation of land-related documents by these individuals or
organizations. To safeguard the University’s interests, it was resolved
that all documents related to land matters—except those in the public
domain (e.g., court judgments)—shall be classified as "Confidential."
These documents will not be disclosed in the interest of the university
till the land is fully alienated and registered in favour of UoH.

6. Request from External Agencies for University Land: The Executive
Council resolved that the University shall not permit any new

earmarking of land at least till the land is alienated in favour of UoH.

land in its favour by the State Government and to achieve this objective,
